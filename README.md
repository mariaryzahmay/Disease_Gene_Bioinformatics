# PART C. Exons,Introns, and Transcript
A. Number of exons
- The selected BRCA1 transcript, NM_007294.4, contains 23 exons.

B. Are there multiple transcripts?
- Yes. BRCA1 has multiple transcript or isoform models. NM_007294.4 was selected as the transcript for this analysis.

C. Difference between an exon and an intron
- an exon is a region of a gene that remains in the mature RNA after RNA splicing and may contain coding sequence. An intron is a non-coding intervening region that is removed from the pre-mRNA during RNA splicing.

D. Relative length of introns and exons
- The introns are generally much longer than the exons in the BRCA1 gene. The exon regions appear as shorter boxer, while the introns are represented by the longer connecting regions in the genome browser.

# PART D.   Genome Browser Tracks

A. Genome annotation track used
- The RefSeq Genes track was used to view BRCA1 gene structure, including its transcript and exon-intron organization

B. ClinVar-related variant marks
- The ClinVar track displays variant markers within the BRCA1 genomic region, allowing clinically reported variants to be located relative to the gene structure.

C. Conservation of regions 
- The BRCA1 region is unusually conserved, indicating that some sequences in this region have been strongly preserved across different species.

D. Location of the conserved region 
- The stronger conservation is observed mainly around exonic regions of BRCA1, although conservation can also occur in nearby non-coding regions
  

E. Why strong conservation can suggest biological importance
- Strong evolutionary conservation suggests that a DNA region has been maintained because changes in it may affect an important biological function.However, conservation alone is not enough to prove that a variant is disease-causing

# PART F. Variant Location and Interpretation

A. Where is the variant located relative to the gene?
The selected variant, NM_007294.4.68_69del (185delAG), is located within the BRCA1 gene on chromosome 17. On GRCh38, ClinVar places it at approximately chr17:43124028–43124029.

B. Is it in an exon, intron, UTR, splice region, or another region?
For the selected NM_007294.4 transcript, the variant is located in exon 2 and affects the coding sequence. ClinVar describes c.68_69del as a frameshift variant.

C. Is it likely coding or non-coding?
It is a coding-region variant for NM_007294.4 because it occurs within the coding sequence and changes the protein reading frame.

D. How might the variant affect the gene or gene product?
The deletion of two nucleotides causes a frameshift beginning at codon 23, producing a premature stop signal and the predicted protein change p.Glu23ValfsTer17. ClinVar classifies this variant as Pathogenic and notes that it is expected to result in an absent or disrupted BRCA1 protein product.

E. What additional evidence is needed before concluding that the variant causes disease?
Additional evidence could include functional studies, population-frequency data, clinical observations, family segregation studies, and previously published research showing that the variant affects BRCA1 function and is associated with disease. A genomic location alone cannot establish pathogenicity.

# PART G. Reflection

1. What did the UCSC show you about your gene that was not obvious from simply reading about the gene’s function?
The UCSC Genome Browser showed the physical organization of BRCA1, including its chromosome location, exon-intron structure, multiple transcripts, conserved regions, and positions of clinically reported variants. This made it easier to see how the gene is organized at the DNA level.

2. Why is knowing the exact genomic location of a disease-associated variant useful?
Knowing the exact genomic location allows researchers to determine whether a variant is located in an exon, intron, UTR, splice region, or another functional region. It also allows the variant to be compared with gene annotations, conservation data, and other genomic information.

3. What is one limitation of predicting a variant’s effect only from its genomic location?
A variant’s location alone cannot determine whether it causes disease because its actual effect depends on factors such as the type of DNA change, its effect on RNA or protein, functional evidence, and clinical data.

4. What was the most interesting feature you observed about your assigned gene?
The most interesting feature was that BRCA1 contains many exons and shows unusually conserved regions across species, suggesting that some parts of the gene have important biological functions. It was also interesting to see a pathogenic ClinVar variant directly positioned within the gene structure.
